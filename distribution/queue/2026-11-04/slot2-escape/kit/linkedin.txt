Why Making Things Harder Makes Better Buyers

Making something harder to buy brings better buyers.

How do you stop wasting time on buyers who never buy? Most people think you need to make checkout faster and easier to grow your customer list.

That assumption costs you thousands in wasted support calls and refunds from the wrong crowd. Here is the friction dial: adding steps and questions cuts total volume.

Why would you intentionally turn away potential sign-ups? Because it filters out tire kickers and leaves only the buyers who actually value the result.

About 1 in 5 US businesses close by year five, often drowning in poor customers. Instead of removing friction from the start, you add hoops once your flow works.

You trade raw volume for high intent by forcing questions before checkout. That exact friction sweet spot protects your time and raises your average order quality.
