The Hot-Hand Fallacy: Why One Win Triggers Ruin

One lucky win feels like skill.

That single lucky win in business or trading triggers a cognitive glitch known as the hot-hand fallacy. Why does a random streak of luck convince smart people to risk their entire net worth on the next gamble?

Most people assume a hot streak means your odds of winning keep getting better with every single wager. That assumption is entirely false.

Independent events never remember what happened on the turn before. When you double down after a win, the mathematical house edge hasn't changed at all.

How does that overconfidence quietly drain an entire savings account in a single afternoon? The brain treats dopamine from a random win the same way it treats proven skill.

Instead of cashing out, you take on massive leverage, treating your luck as a permanent asset. Real wealth isn't built on hot streaks.
