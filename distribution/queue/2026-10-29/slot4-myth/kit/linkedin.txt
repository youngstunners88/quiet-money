The Cost-Per-Use Rule That Saves Thousands

Cheap items are destroying your wealth.

Cheap price tags feel like wins, but why don't cheap items actually save you money? Most people assume picking the lowest price at checkout keeps more cash in their bank account.

But cheap items break fast, forcing endless replacements that quietly destroy your hard-earned net worth. A simple $15 monthly replacement habit burns $1,800 over 10 years without building any real value.

It's called the Cost-Per-Use Rule: total price divided by total days you actually use it. $30 boots replaced yearly cost you 15 cents for every single day you wear them.

A $150 pair lasting ten years costs just 7 cents a day—less than half the cost. What if you invested that daily difference into real assets instead of endless low-quality replacements?

Investing a $5 a day habit at 8% grows to $226,659 over 30 years. Don't fall for low checkout prices when high lifetime costs drain your future wealth.
