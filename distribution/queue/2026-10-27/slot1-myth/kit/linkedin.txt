Why Staying at One Company Costs You 50% in Income

Company loyalty is costing you 50%.

You're taught that staying loyal pays off through bigger promotions and steady annual pay raises. Why does company loyalty cap your paycheck while job switching multiplies your income?

Most workers assume staying put and working hard guarantees the highest long-term salary growth. In reality, internal raises average just 3%, which that's barely keeping pace with 3% inflation.

So how's that tiny 3% raise compounding into a massive 50% lifetime earnings loss? When you switch companies, average job-hop raises jump between 10% and 20% instantly.

But why do corporate payroll budgets aggressively favor outside hires over their most loyal workers? Retention budgets are anchored to your starting salary, strictly capped by rigid corporate policy.

Meanwhile, competitive hiring budgets must match current market rates just to pull in new talent. Company loyalty caps your paycheck because internal raises stay at 3% while hiring budgets pay market rates.

Try this: Reframe your labor as a business service rather than an emotional family loyalty.
