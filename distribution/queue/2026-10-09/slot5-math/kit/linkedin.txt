Why the Last 10 Years Make You Rich

The compound interest snowball trick.

If you invest $500 a month at an 8% return, something unnatural happens after year 30. Think of rolling a tiny snowball right at the top of a steep mountain slope.

At first, each rotation feels slow and pointless, gathering barely a dusting of fresh snow. After 20 years, you've put in $120,000 of your own cash, and you have $294,510.

Two decades of hard work just to double your money. That's where most people give up.

But here's the part nobody mentions. By year 30, your balance reaches $745,180, while you've only contributed $180,000 from your paychecks.

Now your snowball is massive. Every single rotation pulls in a wall of snow.

Try this: It's collected by refusing to step off the ride.

Did you realize over half of your 40-year investment returns show up in the last 10 years?
