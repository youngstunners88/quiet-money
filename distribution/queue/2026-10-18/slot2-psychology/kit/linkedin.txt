Why You Irrationally Overvalue Things You Own

Why we hoard things we don't use.

It's called the endowment effect, and breaking it frees thousands of dollars in trapped cash. In 1990, Nobel economist Richard Thaler proved this irrational bias using simple coffee mugs.

Students given mugs refused to sell for under $7, while buyers wouldn't pay more than $3. Nothing changed about the mug.

The mere feeling of ownership completely distorted their financial judgment. But here's the part nobody mentions: you make this expensive mistake every single week.

You hoard clothes with tags still attached, and keep an extra car bleeding monthly insurance payments. Selling that second vehicle could easily free up an extra $100 every single month.

Invested monthly at 8% a year for 30 years, that $100 compounds to $149,036. Here's your exact counter-move to stop losing money: apply the replacement test.

Try this: Ask yourself: if I didn't own this today, would I spend real cash to buy it right now?
