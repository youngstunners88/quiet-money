How to Build a Personal Monopoly

Stop competing on one skill.

There's always someone cheaper, faster, or willing to work eighty hours a week. The escape isn't working harder.

It's building what economists call a personal monopoly. You don't need to be in the top 1% of your field.

You only need to combine two distinct skills where you're reasonably competent. An average programmer is common.

An average programmer who writes sharp sales copy is rare. When you sit at that intersection, competition disappears and you dictate your terms.

That pricing power creates real leverage outside of your regular paycheck. An extra $1,000/month from a side income, invested at 8% for 10 years, equals $182,946.

Try this: This week, write down your two strongest skills and find three clients needing both.

What are two skills you could combine that nobody else in your workplace has?
