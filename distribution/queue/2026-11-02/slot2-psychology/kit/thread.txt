1/7 Why You Keep Throwing Good Money After Bad

The money pit repair trap

---

2/7 You do it because you spent $3,000 on repairs last year and hate walking away. That's the sunk cost fallacy, a mental trap that confuses past payments with future value. Most people think pouring more cash in protects their past investment.

---

3/7 In reality, every dollar you spent yesterday is gone forever and cannot be recovered. Why do smart earners double down on dying assets instead of cutting their losses?

---

4/7 Your brain hates recognizing a loss, so it treats write-offs like personal failures. So instead of accepting a $3,000 loss today, you lock in a $5,000 loss tomorrow. What's the simple counter-move that stops this quiet drain on your paycheck?

---

5/7 Step one is evaluating an asset strictly on its current market utility, ignoring yesterday's spending. If a repair costs more than the asset's utility, walk away and redirect that cash elsewhere.

---
