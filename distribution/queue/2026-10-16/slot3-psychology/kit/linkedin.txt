The Decoy Effect: Why You Buy the Most Expensive Option

You didn't want the large size.

That middle option wasn't built to sell. It's an engineered trap designed to steal your cash.

Psychologists call this the decoy effect, and retailers use it everywhere to quietly dictate your choices. Imagine a small coffee costs $3, and a large costs $7.

Most people pick the $3 cup. Now, the menu adds a medium for $6.50.

Suddenly, the $7 large feels like an absolute bargain. But here's the part nobody mentions.

The company never expected anyone to buy that $6.50 medium. It exists purely to frame the large.

Try this: Most people pick the $3 cup.

Have you ever upgraded to the large just because the medium was only 50 cents less?
