The Raise Math Nobody Teaches You

Why a raise feels smaller.

People think crossing into a higher tax bracket means losing money on your entire salary. That is completely false.

Marginal brackets only apply to the dollars sitting above that specific income line. Marginal tax brackets mean you always take home more when you earn more, but supplemental withholding on bonuses can temporarily take a bigger bite upfront.

When your employer pays a bonus, federal rules often mandate a flat twenty-two percent withholding rate. If that flat rate sits higher than your normal tax bracket, your net payout looks noticeably lighter than expected.

You don't lose that money permanently—any over-withholding comes back as a tax refund when you file. Understanding this cash flow dip stops you from panicking when your annual bonus hits your account.

Always check your yearly tax return to ensure your total withholding matches your actual liability. Mastering the mechanics of your paycheck keeps you in control of your financial growth.
