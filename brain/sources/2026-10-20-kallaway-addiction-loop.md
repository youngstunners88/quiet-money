# The four-step story loop

## In one line
Attention holds when a story runs stakes, big question, head fake, re-hook; great stories chain several loops.

## Claims
| # | Claim | Label | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Four steps: stakes, big question, head fake, re-hook | S | his framework |
| 2 | Stakes = a character to root for + something at risk + urgency; only need to be personally relevant | S | |
| 3 | Stakes earn attention; the big question creates the curiosity that keeps people watching | S | |
| 4 | Head fake: lead the viewer to assume A, reveal B | S | contrast |
| 5 | Re-hook fast; great stories cascade loops | S | |
| 6 | "Scientifically impossible to skip", "same psychology casinos use" | U | hype, no evidence |
| 7 | His long video has over 100,000 views | U | self-reported |

## Tactics
Character + risk + clock in the first line. One question planted on purpose. A reversal against the expectation
the opening created. The next question opened right after the payoff.

## How Quiet Money could use it
1. script-lab: map onto our 60-second structure. Stakes go in the hook (0-3 s), the big question in the open loop
   (3-8 s), the head fake in the mid pattern interrupt, the re-hook in the loop ending. New parts: the hook names a
   person, a risk and a time; the reversal contradicts what the first 8 s made you assume; a second, smaller
   question opens right after the first payoff (about 30-40 s).
2. faceless-marketing: story is our best measured pillar (best Short 176 views, a founder story; median 16 across
   32 Shorts). Pre-register X12: loop-structured story scripts vs current ones, 10 per arm, median 48-h views and
   average % viewed. Kill: no lift after 10 per arm, keep control.
3. faceless-gauntlet (shadow first): judge items. "Beat 1 names a character, a risk and a time." "One explicit
   question by 8 s." "A true reversal between 25 and 45 s." "The last line opens the next loop."
4. Example with facts already in channel/brand.md. Stakes: "A janitor died with $8 million. And nobody knew."
   Question: "How does a janitor's paycheck become $8 million?" Head fake: "You'd expect a lucky tip..."; the real
   answer comes only from the research brief.

## Reject
- Casino or addiction as the goal. Our viewers feel behind with money and many fight compulsive loops. We borrow
  curiosity and reversal, not "you can't stop watching".
- Invented stakes about the viewer's money ("you'll lose your house in two weeks"): fake urgency and outcome claims (MARKETING.md section 11).
- A head fake that is bait-and-switch. The reversal must be the documented fact (rules 1, 8).
- "Comment a keyword and I'll DM it": no DM automation, Shorts comments are not tappable, one offer (rule 13).
- Repeating claims 6 and 7 as facts.

## Open questions or tests
