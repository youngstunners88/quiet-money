# Follow-up sequences (3 steps after the first touch, 5 only for a retainer-sized prospect)

Each step must **add something**, never ask again. "Bumping this" is a rejected draft. Stop the whole sequence the
moment they reply, on any channel, or ask not to be contacted.

| Step | Days after previous | Job | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | Give proof | One named example closest to their niche + the portfolio index link. Two sentences. |
| 2 | 7 | Give an idea | The actual hook you would use for their next dated thing, written out. Three sentences. No ask. |
| 3 | 7 | Close the loop | "I'll leave it here - if video comes up this quarter, the index is [link]." One sentence, no question. |
| 4 (retainer only) | 14 | New trigger | Only if something new happened on their side (a dated event, a raise, a new product). Reference it. |
| 5 (retainer only) | 21 | Final | Same as step 3's shape. Then the lead is `dead` for 90 days. |

## Hard stops
- Three steps for most leads. Five only when the lead card shows a budget signal **and** fit scored high.
- Never more than one message per channel per week to the same project.
- A `reply-negative`, an unsubscribe request or a complaint ends the sequence permanently and is recorded as an
  outcome. No "just checking if things changed" months later.
- If a step cannot say something new, the sequence ends early. An early end is a better outcome than a bump.
