# LeBron James
Researched 2026-10-30. Use only what is below; anything else about him needs a new source first.
How to use: neuron W1 (equity over wages). The video is about one decision and what it did, not the man. Say "reportedly" or "Forbes estimates" where marked. Never show his face or likeness.

## Verified facts (attribute them)
- In 2015 he did not renew his McDonald's endorsement (started 2010, extended in 2012 for roughly $4 million a year). His business partner Maverick Carter said that meant walking away from about $14-15 million over four years. He put his weight behind Blaze Pizza instead (Forbes, Kurt Badenhausen, 2017-04-26).
- He was an early investor in Blaze Pizza in 2012. The first investment was about $1 million (PitchBook), or less than $1 million (ESPN, as cited by Investopedia), made with his partners. ESPN's Darren Rovell reported he owns more than 10% (via Forbes 2017). He and partners also hold franchise rights (17 Blaze franchises in 2017, per Forbes).
- Blaze stake value: Forbes estimated it at about $30 million in 2022 (CNBC, 2022-06-02). A 2017 private-equity deal for a stake in Blaze at about $250 million led to reports that his stake was worth about $35 million (PitchBook, 2018). These are estimates, not sales.
- Beats by Dre: he got a small stake in 2008, at the company's start, in exchange for promoting the headphones. Apple bought Beats for $3 billion in 2014. He reportedly made about $30 million in cash and stock, said to be the largest payout to a pro athlete at the time (SBN 2015; PitchBook 2018).
- Nike: his first Nike deal was worth more than $100 million over seven years, including bonuses and royalties (Forbes 2018). In late 2015 he signed a lifetime deal that Maverick Carter says will pay at least $1 billion. James himself never confirmed the figure (Forbes 2018; CNBC 2022).
- His first NBA contract, with the Cleveland Cavaliers, was $18.8 million over four years (Forbes 2018).
- SpringHill Company: started 2020 with a $100 million investment (Bloomberg, via Investopedia). In 2021 it sold a minority stake at a valuation of about $725 million (Forbes). Forbes estimates his stake at about $300 million.
- Fenway Sports Group: in 2021 he and Carter swapped their Liverpool FC stake for a stake in Fenway Sports Group. Forbes estimates he owns about 1%, worth about $90 million (Investopedia, 2023).
- Forbes named him the first active NBA player to become a billionaire (CNBC, 2022-06-02). Forbes put his career pretax earnings near $1.3 billion in 2023, and his 2022-23 salary and on-court pay at $124.5 million, so he earned a very large paycheck as well (Forbes, 2023-02-08).

## The honest catch (every video names one)
- He did not pick equity instead of money in general. He was one of the highest-paid players alive, and his equity sat on top of that income.
- He put money in himself (about $1 million in Blaze), and he was famous enough that companies wanted his name. Most people are offered neither.
- Many equity deals pay nothing; we only hear about the ones that paid. Stake values above are Forbes estimates, and Blaze's value can fall as well as rise.
- Beats: his payout came from a company that sold for $3 billion. Say what he received, not what the company was worth to him.

## Do not say
- "$700 million" from Beats. A former teammate said this on a podcast in 2020 and no source supports it.
- That he took "zero dollars upfront" or "gave up a paycheck for nothing". He walked away from reported endorsement money and invested his own.
- Any net worth figure without a dated Forbes source, or any claim about what Blaze or SpringHill are worth today.
- That he owns Liverpool FC. He held a minority stake, later swapped into Fenway Sports Group.
- That this works for the viewer. Say what he did and what it cost him.
- Imply he endorses, sponsors, or knows this channel. Quote him.
