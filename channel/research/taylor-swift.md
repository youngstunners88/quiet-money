# Taylor Swift
Researched 2026-10-30. Use only what is below; anything else about her needs a new source first.
How to use: neuron W2 (own the master). The video is about one decision: who owns the recording. Never show her face or likeness.

## Verified facts (attribute them)
- She signed with Big Machine at 15 (2005). As was customary, the label kept the master recordings (The Guardian, 2025-05-30).
- 2019-06-30: Scooter Braun's Ithaca Holdings agreed to buy Big Machine Label Group, and with it the masters of her first six albums, for around $300 million (Variety, 2025). She called it her "worst case scenario" (Deadline, The Verge).
- Her account: for years she asked for a chance to own her work and was told she could "earn" one album back for each new album she made for the label (The Guardian; The Verge). Braun has said he offered to sell the catalog back and her team refused (Deadline). The two accounts conflict; present the sale as "she says".
- 2020-11: Braun sold the catalog to Shamrock Capital. Variety reported $300 million from sources; Billboard (via The Verge and NPR) reported about $360 million. Use "reportedly $300 million or more".
- She re-recorded her old albums as "Taylor's Version": Fearless, Red, Speak Now and 1989 were out by May 2025 (Reuters; Deadline). She had kept the publishing to her songs, because she writes them, which let her re-record (NPR).
- 2025-05-30: she announced she had bought the masters of her first six albums back from Shamrock Capital, plus her music videos, concert films and artwork (Reuters; The Verge). The price was not disclosed; Variety and Deadline say nine figures. A Variety source said it was closer to $300 million than to the higher numbers some outlets printed, while Billboard reported about $360 million.
- In her words she bought the music "outright with no strings attached, no partnership, with full autonomy" (letter to fans, quoted by Variety and The Guardian).
- She credited fan support of the re-recordings and The Eras Tour for making the purchase possible (The Verge).

## The honest catch (every video names one)
- Getting the masters back took six years, a public fight and hundreds of millions of dollars. It was possible because of her fame; most artists never get that chance.
- The real lesson for the viewer is the contract: who owns the work you make, and what has to happen for it to come back.
- Prices here are reported, not confirmed. She also did not own these masters when they were made; she chose to sign the standard deal at 15.

## Do not say
- "$330 million" for the 2019 sale (the sources say about $300 million).
- A single price for the 2025 purchase.
- That Scooter Braun took part in the 2025 sale (a source close to the deal said he did not).
- That ownership "made" her rich; use "in this case".
