# Robert Kiyosaki
Researched 2026-10-02. Use only what is below; anything else about him needs a new source first.
How to use: teach the FRAMEWORKS in our own words. No quotes. The video is about the method, not the person; naming them is optional and never the hook. The facts below exist so anything we do say is true.

## Verified facts (attribute them)
- Born 1947 in Hilo, Hawaii. His father, the "poor dad" of the book, was Hawaii's superintendent of schools.
- Rich Dad Poor Dad came out in 1997 and had sold close to 40 million copies by 2017.
- Rich Dad's CASHFLOW Quadrant followed in 2000. He also sells the CASHFLOW board game, whose goal is to
  "get out of the rat race".
- In 2012 his company Rich Global LLC filed for bankruptcy after a court ordered it to pay $23.7 million to
  The Learning Annex.
- In January 2024 he said he had more than $1 billion in debt and called it "not my problem".
- Critics, notably real-estate author John T. Reed, argue the "rich dad" character was probably invented and
  call some of the book's advice dangerous.

## Frameworks (explain in plain words, credit him)
- Asset vs liability: an asset puts money in your pocket every month; a liability takes money out.
  By that definition a house you live in is a liability while it costs you money each month.
- The rat race: you earn, your expenses rise with your income, and you need the next paycheck.
  You're out when your passive income covers your monthly expenses.
- CASHFLOW Quadrant: E (employee), S (self-employed), B (business owner), I (investor). The left side trades time
  for money; the right side owns systems and assets that earn without you.
- Pay yourself first: move money to savings and investments before paying bills.
- Good debt buys things that pay you (like a rental that cash-flows); bad debt buys things that cost you.

## The honest catch (every video names one)
- Leverage cuts both ways: debt that buys assets also magnifies losses, and his own company went bankrupt.
- "Passive" income takes capital or years of work to build; real estate needs repairs, tenants, and vacancies.
- His story's accuracy is disputed; teach the idea, not the legend.

## Never
- Present his market predictions as facts or tell viewers to buy gold, silver, or bitcoin because he does.
- Quote him. Show his face. Promise that following him makes anyone rich.
