# Jay-Z (Shawn Carter)
Researched 2026-10-30. Use only what is below; anything else about him needs a new source first.
How to use: neuron W2 (own the master) and W1. W5 (quiet wealth) does not fit him: his brands are loud by design. The video is about one decision, not the man. Never show his face or likeness.

## Verified facts (attribute them)
- 1996: no major label would sign him, so he co-founded Roc-A-Fella Records and released his debut himself. In 1997 Def Jam bought half of Roc-A-Fella for $1.5 million; in 2004 Def Jam bought the rest for $10 million (Pitchfork, 2017).
- Masters: in a 2023 CBS Mornings interview he said that going back to Def Jam as president came with the condition that his masters revert to him, and that he will not sell them. Pitchfork (2017) reports his Def Jam contract gave him ownership of his catalog from 2014. In 2009 he left Def Jam, reportedly at a cost of $5 million, and gained control of his future master recordings (Reuters, 2009). The sources differ on timing; do not state a single year.
- 2008: a roughly $150 million, 10-year deal with Live Nation that created Roc Nation; Live Nation filed to give his company 775,434 shares (SEC Form 8-K, via Billboard and The Hollywood Reporter, 2008).
- Armand de Brignac ("Ace of Spades"): he took full ownership in 2014 (FT, 2021). On 2021-02-22 he sold 50% to Moët Hennessy (LVMH) for an undisclosed price. The brand sold 500,000 bottles in 2019 (FT). Forbes estimated the brand at about $630 million and his cash from the sale at "at least $300 million"; this is Forbes' estimate, not a disclosed figure (Forbes, 2021-02-26).
- Tidal: an artist group he led bought it in 2015 for $56 million. In March 2021 Square (now Block) agreed to pay $297 million in cash and stock for a "significant majority" stake, and he joined Square's board (The Verge and NYT, 2021-03-04). Forbes valued Tidal at about $450 million and estimated he netted about $149 million in cash and stock; the second figure is a Forbes estimate.
- He held an early stake in Uber (Pitchfork, 2017). The size is not public: state no number.
- Forbes called him hip-hop's first billionaire in 2019; confirm with the Forbes page before saying it.

## The honest catch (every video names one)
- He started with a hit record and famous partners, and he won his masters in a negotiation few artists could have had.
- The two big sales came in the same two weeks in 2021, during a pandemic downturn that hit his other businesses (NYT); one reading is that he sold to raise cash.
- Valuations here are estimates. Several of his businesses (clubs, clothing, other brands) did not become giants.

## Do not say
- That he still owns all of his champagne brand (he sold half in 2021) or that he fully owns D'Ussé (not checked; leave out).
- A single year for when his masters returned, or the price of the Uber stake.
- "Forbes says he made $300 million" without "at least" and "estimated".
- Any quote of his beyond what is paraphrased here. Imply he endorses, sponsors, or knows this channel.
