## Joins and the four dimensions (Film Art)
- Shots join by cut (the common one), fade, dissolve or wipe. Fades, dissolves and wipes are optical effects; a dissolve overlaps two shots, a wipe replaces one by a moving line.
- An edit controls four relations between shot A and shot B: **graphic** (shapes, light, colour: match for smoothness or clash for shock), **rhythmic** (length, accent, beat, tempo), **spatial** (any two places can be related by similarity, difference or development) and **temporal** (order, duration, ellipsis, flashback).
- **Kuleshov effect:** shots without an establishing shot make the viewer infer one space; the same face means different things after soup, a coffin or a child.
- **Parallel editing or crosscutting** alternates lines of action in different places (Griffith's chases and rescues).
- **Ellipsis:** show less time than the story takes, using a dissolve, a fade, a cutaway or a black frame.
- **Continuity system:** keep graphics roughly continuous, make cut length depend on camera distance (long shots stay longer than mediums, mediums longer than close-ups), and hold the 180-degree line so positions, eyelines and screen direction stay stable. Eyeline match, match on action, crosscutting. Time is organised by cause and effect, curiosity, suspense and surprise.
- **Alternatives:** graphic and rhythmic cutting (Brakhage, Busby Berkeley), spatial and temporal discontinuity (Tati, Ozu), jump cuts (Godard), non-diegetic inserts (the slaughtered cow in Eisenstein's *Strike*), and collision montage (the Odessa Steps in *Battleship Potemkin*), where conflict between shots makes the viewer think.

## History that matters (Dancyger ch. 1 and IJRAR)
- Early film had no editing; each shot was a tableau. **Porter (1903)** showed that a shot is the unit of a film and that shots from different places, joined, mean more than their sum; entering a shot mid-action implies time passed, leaving early implies a change of place.
- **Griffith** made editing dramatic: close-ups to move into emotion, cutaways, parallel action with shots getting shorter toward the rescue, extreme long shots for scale, dramatic time replacing real time.
- **Pudovkin** (constructive editing): the director builds "filmic time and space" from fragments; show the prisoner's joy by hands, a smile, a brook, birds, a child, not by one face.
- **Eisenstein's montage:** metric (shot length), rhythmic (visual pattern, direction), tonal (the emotional character), overtonal (all three together), intellectual (an idea inserted: Kerensky climbing stairs cut with a preening mechanical peacock).
- **Vertov:** the honest, playful, self-aware edit. **Dovzhenko:** editing by visual association, like a poem. **Buñuel:** deliberate visual discontinuity to unsettle.
- **Early sound:** the 1928 manifesto (Eisenstein, Pudovkin, Alexandrov) argued for asynchronous, contrapuntal sound so montage survived; sound kept the single shot from becoming a filmed play. Technique (the join), craft (the join that makes a meaning neither shot has alone) and art (the join that reaches excitement, insight, shock or discovery) are Dancyger's three levels.
- An editor has succeeded when the audience forgets the juxtaposition and enjoys the story; editors hold ethical power because emotion shapes meaning.

## Practical notes (IJRAR)
Log and organise footage so rhythm is never broken by hunting for a shot; build the cut linearly, keep an edit decision list, and use non-linear editing's freedom without losing the craft of choosing by removal. Time-code and slates make synchronisation simple. Music-driven editing (as in pop videos) is a legitimate base for a cut.
