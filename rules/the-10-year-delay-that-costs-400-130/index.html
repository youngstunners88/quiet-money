The full story

Waiting ten years to start investing will cost you exactly $400,130. It's not because you missed a hot tech stock, but because of a quiet math rule they never teach you. Let's compare two different people, both saving exactly $200 every single month at an 8% annual return. The first person starts early at age 25. They invest their $200 every single month without fail.

By age 65, their total out-of-pocket contributions over 40 years add up to $96,000. But monthly compounding quietly turns that modest sum into a massive $698,202. But here's the part nobody mentions about waiting. The second person waits until age 35 to start doing the exact same thing.

By age 65, they've put in $72,000, which is only $24,000 less than the first person. Yet, because they had less time to let compounding work, their final balance stops at only $298,072. That 10-year delay cost them $400,130 in pure, effortless wealth. Follow for the money rules school never taught you, so you don't end up silently...
