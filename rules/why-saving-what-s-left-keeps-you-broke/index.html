Why Saving What's Left Keeps You Broke

Why waiting until the end of the month to save guarantees you will stay broke, and the pay-yourself-first rule that changes everything.

The rule: Stop saving what's left at the end.

The full story

Saving whatever money is left at the end of the month is a trap. It assumes there will actually be something left over. But expenses always expand to fill your paycheck. That is why most people reach age 65 with nothing saved. They treat saving as an afterthought. Here is the exact rule that flips the script entirely. It is called paying yourself first.

The second your paycheck hits your account, a set amount moves straight into investments. Before bills. You force yourself to live on what is left, rather than saving what is left. Look at the raw math when you make this simple shift early on in life. Investing $200 a month at 8% a year for 40 years grows into $698,202.

While your total out-of-pocket deposits are only $96,000 over that entire span. Wait until you see what happens if you delay that exact same habit by ten years. Starting at 35 instead of 25 drops that final balance down to $298,072. That one decade of delay costs you over $400,000 in missed compounding.
