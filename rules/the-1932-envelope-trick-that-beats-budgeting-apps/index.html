The full story

In 1932, broke families survived the Great Depression using cheap paper envelopes. It's a forgotten physical hack that still outperforms every modern budgeting app. During the worst economic collapse in US history, average household income fell by 40%. Yet, families who partitioned physical cash into labeled pockets survived without going into debt.

One envelope held rent money, another held grocer cash, a third held coal coins. When an envelope was empty, they stopped spending. The boundary was absolute. Modern plastic cards remove this physical friction, tricking your brain into overspending by 100%. But here's the part nobody mentions about running this system digitally today.

Step one is setting up three free, targeted sub-accounts with your bank. Step two is automating your paycheck to split directly into those digital vaults. Step three is deleting the banking apps that make transferring too easy. You recreate the painful physical limits of the 1930s without carrying paper cash.
