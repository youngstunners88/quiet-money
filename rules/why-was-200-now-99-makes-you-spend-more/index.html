The full story

The first number you see controls your decision, even if it's completely fake. Stores use this trick on every single tag, and it's called anchoring. When you see a jacket marked down from $200 to $99, your brain grabs that first high figure. That $200 becomes your anchor, making $99 feel like an absolute steal.

Even if the item's true value is only $40, you still think you're saving money. Your brain is literally wired to compare, not to calculate actual worth. Retailers know this, and that's why crossed-out prices are everywhere you look. But here's the part nobody mentions about how this quietly drains your bank account.

You end up buying things you don't need just because the discount looks massive. The counter-move is simple: ignore the first number and ask yourself one question.
