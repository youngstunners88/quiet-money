The Bi-Weekly Mortgage Hack That Erases 8 Years

The full story

Changing one payment date on your mortgage can erase 8 years of debt. Most homeowners pay their bill once a month, right on the first. How does splitting that exact same paycheck in half cut your loan down fast? You assume paying bi-weekly just spreads your normal bill evenly throughout the year.

But calendar math hides a powerful twist most homeowners never calculate for themselves. There are 52 weeks in a year, giving you 26 half-payments every two weeks. 26 half-payments equal 13 full monthly payments every year, instead of just 12. But where does that sudden 13th payment go, and how does it help you?

That extra payment hits your principal directly, skipping interest costs that normally compound against you. That single adjustment turns a 30-year loan into a 22-year loan automatically. Call your loan provider today and ask for an automated bi-weekly payment schedule. You spend the exact same monthly budget, but save tens of thousands in interest.

Sources and math

  • Paying bi-weekly results in 26 half-payments, which equals 13 full monthly payments per year. (52 weeks divided by 2 = 26 half-payments = 13 full payments.)
  • Paying one extra full payment toward mortgage principal per year reduces a standard 30-year loan to approximately 22 years. (Standard amortization calculations for a 30-year fixed rate mortgage.)
