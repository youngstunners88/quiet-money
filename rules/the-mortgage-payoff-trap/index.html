The Mortgage Payoff Trap

Paying off a low-interest mortgage early often destroys long-term wealth through missed compounding opportunities. Here is the comparative math behind cheap debt.

The rule: Paying off your mortgage early is a trap.

The full story

Throwing extra cash at a low-interest mortgage feels smart. It's actually costing you hundreds of thousands. That fixed rate debt is cheap. Meanwhile, missing out on compounding interest destroys your net worth. Take investing $500 a month at 8% a year. In 30 years, it grows into $745,180. If you send that money to the bank instead, you save a tiny 3% or 4% interest rate.

You're trading a massive compounding return for a tiny guaranteed savings. Look at the math. In 40 years, that same $500 a month turns into $1,745,504. That house is paid off, but your overall wealth is miles behind where it could be. Inflation also eats away at that debt. Your fixed payment shrinks in real buying power every single year.
