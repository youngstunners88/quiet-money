Why do we spend a $2,000 tax refund three times faster than our regular paycheck? Behavioral economists call this mental accounting, and it quietly drains thousands from your net worth. Why does your brain treat incoming cash differently depending on where it comes from? You assume a refund is free bonus money because it didn't come from daily work. In reality, it's just an interest-free loan you gave the government from your own earnings. So what actually happens when you blow that money while carrying high-interest credit card debt? A $3,000 credit card balance at 24% APR paying only minimums takes 15 years to pay off. Sticking to minimum payments burns $4,887 in total interest on that single $3,000 balance. So how do you stop treating unexpected money like play cash before you blow it? Set up single-bucket routing: send every refund, bonus, and cash gift straight to one buffer account. When every dollar lands in the exact same place, your brain stops finding excuses to waste it. Follow for the money rules school never taught you, and keep every dollar working for your future.
