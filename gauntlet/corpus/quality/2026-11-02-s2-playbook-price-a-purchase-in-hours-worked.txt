That $100 shirt doesn't cost $100. It costs half a day of your life. Before you buy it, turn the price into hours of your work. Step one. Work out your exact take-home pay per hour once. Divide your monthly take-home pay by the total hours you spend working. Step two. Divide the price of that new item by your actual hourly take-home rate. Is that item really worth the exact hours you spent earning it? Step three. Ask whether the thing is worth those hours of labor. Most people assume a $200 purchase is just $200 from an abstract account. The truth is you traded actual living time for that specific object. Money is just stored human time sitting in your wallet. When you price things in hours, impulse buys suddenly lose their shiny appeal. Calculate your last impulse buy right now and test this rule today. Follow for the money rules school never taught you.
