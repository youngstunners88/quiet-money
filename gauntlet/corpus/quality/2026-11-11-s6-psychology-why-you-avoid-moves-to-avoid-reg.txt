Why does doing nothing feel so much safer than making a reasonable move? You assume you're being cautious, protecting yourself from making an embarrassing mistake. Psychologists call it anticipated regret. Your brain fears a bad action twice as much as inaction. But what does dodging that regret actually cost your bank account over time? Take $200 a month invested at 8% from age 25 to 65. It grows to $698,202. Wait 10 years to start at 35 because you feared picking the wrong fund. At 65, that same $200 a month reaches only $298,072. Where did that missing $400,130 go when you never lost a single dollar in the market? Inaction is still an action. You traded a temporary feeling of comfort for a $400,130 penalty. A simple, decent move made early beats a flawless move made 10 years too late. Next time, don't close the tab. Follow for the money rules school never taught you.
