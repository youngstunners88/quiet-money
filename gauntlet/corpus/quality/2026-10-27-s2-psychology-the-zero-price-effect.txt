Add a $5 shipping fee and you'll abandon your cart instantly. Slap on free shipping for a $70 item you don't need, and you buy it immediately. Most people think they're just saving $5 and getting a smart deal. But researchers call this the Zero-Price Effect, where zero turns off your brain's fear of loss. In a famous MIT experiment, people picked cheap free chocolate over a luxury truffle at a massive discount. When something costs zero, your brain overvalues the item and ignores the hidden trade-off.
