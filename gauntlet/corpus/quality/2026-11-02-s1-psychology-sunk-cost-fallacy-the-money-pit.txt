Putting a $2,000 transmission into a $1,500 car quietly drains your wealth. You do it because you spent $3,000 on repairs last year and hate walking away. That's the sunk cost fallacy, a mental trap that confuses past payments with future value. Most people think pouring more cash in protects their past investment. In reality, every dollar you spent yesterday is gone forever and cannot be recovered. Why do smart earners double down on dying assets instead of cutting their losses? Your brain hates recognizing a loss, so it treats write-offs like personal failures. So instead of accepting a $3,000 loss today, you lock in a $5,000 loss tomorrow. What's the simple counter-move that stops this quiet drain on your paycheck? Step one is evaluating an asset strictly on its current market utility, ignoring yesterday's spending. If a repair costs more than the asset's utility, walk away and redirect that cash elsewhere. That extra $200 a month invested at 8% for 30 years grows to $298,072 instead. Follow Quiet Money for the money rules school never taught you, so you never drain your wealth.
