In 1720, a convicted murderer took over France's entire economy and printed paper out of thin air. His name was John Law, and he promised paper notes would replace gold. Stay to see how it ends. Law founded the Mississippi Company, claiming America's Louisiana territory held mountains of pure gold. Citizens rushed to swap hard coins for shares. Stocks jumped from 500 livres to 10,000 livres. Ordinary folks became rich overnight. Waiters bought estates, and coachmen drove past their former masters. But here's the part nobody mentions. The gold in Louisiana didn't exist. It was an illusion. In late 1720, smart investors tried cashing out their paper shares for actual silver coins. The central bank ran out of silver in days. Panic hit Paris like a tidal wave. Share values collapsed to zero. Thousands of families lost every single cent they owned. John Law fled France in disguise, penniless and hunted by the mobs he once made rich. Manias repeat every single generation under new names.
