She bought stock once in 1935, never traded again, and beat Wall Street. Her name was Grace Groner, and her single decision reveals why active trading constantly loses money. Working as an ordinary clerk during the Great Depression, Grace invested exactly $180 into 3 shares. She didn't check financial news, hire expensive advisors, or panic whenever the market took a dive. Instead, she set one automated rule: every dividend check went directly back into buying more shares. But here's the crucial part that modern traders always miss. Across 75 years, through world wars, brutal recessions, and crashes, she didn't sell a single share. While hyperactive investors paid high fees and jumped between stocks, Grace let compounding run in silence. By 2010, that modest $180 stake had quietly expanded into over 100,000 shares worth $7,000,000. She didn't beat the market by outsmarting it; she won because she never interrupted the compounding process. Constant trading feels like productivity, but quiet patience is what builds life-changing wealth over long horizons. Follow for the money rules school never taught you, and let patience do the heavy lifting.
