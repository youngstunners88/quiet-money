Your credit limit is the bank's number, not yours. When a card issuer hands you a $6,000 limit, it feels like permission to spend $6,000. Most people assume a bank only offers a limit your paycheck can easily carry. That's the head fake. A credit limit isn't your safety line; it's the lender's risk cap. Lenders profit when you carry debt, so their ceiling is built to test your limits. So what actually happens if you spend that $6,000 limit and pay minimums? On a $6,000 card at 24% APR, minimum payments take 21 years to clear. Even worse, that original $6,000 debt costs you $10,887 in pure interest. So how do you stop a bank's high limit from quietly draining your financial future? First, ignore the bank's limit and set your own spending line from take-home pay. Second, treat your credit card strictly like debit, paying off every balance in full. Your monthly budget must be set by cash flow, never by a lender's algorithm. Follow for the money rules school never taught you, so the bank's number never becomes yours.
