He owned the Philadelphia Eagles and a massive trucking empire, yet lost it all betting $10,000 hands in Atlantic City. How does a multimillionaire sport owner watch his entire fortune vanish across a single green felt table? Most people assume smart business owners know when to walk away from a losing streak. They think intelligence protects you from the psychology of chasing sunken money. The truth is brutal. Leonard Tose dropped over $40 million because every loss triggered the urge to win it back. Why did doubling his bets feel like control when it was actually financial suicide? Because our brains treat past losses as a debt the universe owes us. He didn't lose because he was dumb. He lost because he couldn't accept a bad trade. That illusion of control destroys wealth faster than any bad investment ever could. Discipline means cutting your losses before they cut your life. Master your losses, or your losses will master you. Follow for the money rules school never taught you.
