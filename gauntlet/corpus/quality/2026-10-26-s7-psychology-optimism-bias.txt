You budget like your transmission, your teeth, and your roof have lifetime warranties. Psychologists call this optimism bias: the conviction that bad luck only strikes other people. Why do we treat routine wear and tear like shocking acts of God? Most people assume a budget only covers predictable rent, groceries, and monthly subscriptions. In reality, brake pads thin out, pipes burst, and molars crack on a predictable timeline. So what happens when an ignored $3,000 car repair hits an empty savings account? It gets dumped onto a credit card charging a brutal 24% interest rate. Paying only the minimum payment keeps you trapped for 183 long months. That single $3,000 surprise ends up costing you $4,887 in interest alone. By the Rule of 72, at 24%, unmanaged debt doubles every 3 years. How do you break this cycle before your emergency fund gets wiped out? The counter-move is simple: stop calling routine maintenance an unexpected emergency. Build monthly sinking funds for future repairs so you never borrow to fix them. Expect the breakdown early, and follow for the money rules school never taught you.
