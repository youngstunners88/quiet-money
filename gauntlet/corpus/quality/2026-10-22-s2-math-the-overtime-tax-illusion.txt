That extra 10 hour shift feels like free money until you see your net paycheck. You worked 50 hours, but your take-home pay barely budged. Where'd that cash actually go? Most workers assume they bumped into a higher tax bracket that eats their entire income. But that's not how tax brackets work. The real culprit is sitting in your HR payroll software. Payroll systems don't know you only worked overtime once. They treat that check like your new weekly salary. By multiplying your temporary pay bump across 52 weeks, the software forces high upfront tax withholding. Some employers even hold back a flat 22% federal tax right off the top for supplemental pay. So if your immediate cash take-home drops, are you simply working those extra hours for minimum wage? You do get that over-withheld cash back at tax time, but upfront, your immediate liquidity shrinks fast. That means your extra weekend hours are giving the government an interest-free loan until April. Knowing payroll withholding math helps you manage your W-4 and protect your real hourly rate. Follow for the money rules school never taught you, so every extra shift actually pays off.
