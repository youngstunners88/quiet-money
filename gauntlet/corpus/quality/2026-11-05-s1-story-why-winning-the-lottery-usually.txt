In 1985 and 1986, Evelyn Adams won $5.4 million in the New Jersey lottery. By 2001, every dollar was gone, leaving her broke in a trailer. How did $5.4 million evaporate? You'd think she wasted it all on mansions, yachts, and expensive sports cars like typical millionaires. But luxury wasn't the main cause. She fell into a psychological trap called the obligation spiral. What is it? Relatives, friends, and strangers begged for loans and handouts she didn't know how to refuse. To cover those losses, she gambled heavily at Atlantic City slot machines, losing millions more trying to win. Guilt bled her cash, while panic broke her judgment. Why does sudden wealth destroy people who aren't prepared? Without clear rules, sudden money magnifies your existing habits, bad advice, and social guilt into total bankruptcy. $5.4 million evaporated because cash without personal boundaries creates endless demands and desperation you can't survive. Windfalls don't fix money problems—they only multiply whatever habits you already have. Build strict financial boundaries today. Follow for the money rules school never taught you.
