The moment an object becomes yours, your brain automatically doubles its perceived value. It's called the endowment effect, and breaking it frees thousands of dollars in trapped cash. In 1990, Nobel economist Richard Thaler proved this irrational bias using simple coffee mugs. Students given mugs refused to sell for under $7, while buyers wouldn't pay more than $3. Nothing changed about the mug. The mere feeling of ownership completely distorted their financial judgment. But here's the part nobody mentions: you make this expensive mistake every single week. You hoard clothes with tags still attached, and keep an extra car bleeding monthly insurance payments. Selling that second vehicle could easily free up an extra $100 every single month. Invested monthly at 8% a year for 30 years, that $100 compounds to $149,036. Here's your exact counter-move to stop losing money: apply the replacement test. Ask yourself: if I didn't own this today, would I spend real cash to buy it right now? If the answer's no, it's just clutter draining your net worth. Sell it and invest the proceeds. Stop letting phantom value trap your future. Follow for the money rules school never taught you.
