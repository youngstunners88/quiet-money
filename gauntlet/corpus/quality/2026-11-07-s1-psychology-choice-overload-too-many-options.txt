Comparing every savings account on the market is how you end up opening none. Psychologists call this paralysis choice overload, and it quietly drains your long-term wealth. When you're faced with dozens of options, why does your brain freeze instead of choosing one? Most people assume gathering more research and comparing every rate leads to a better financial decision. It doesn't. Having too many choices creates friction, turning a simple goal into total decision fatigue. So what does waiting 10 years to make up your mind actually cost your bank account? If you invest $200 a month at 8% starting at age 25, you reach $698,202 by 65. Wait a decade until 35 while comparing options, and that exact same monthly habit yields $298,072. That 10-year delay costs you $400,130 in missing compound growth. To beat choice overload, use the shortlist strategy: narrow your choices to just three solid options. Then pick based on one single rule, like the lowest fee or highest interest rate. Imperfection today beats perfect research tomorrow every single time. Follow for the money rules school never taught you, so you don't end up opening none.
