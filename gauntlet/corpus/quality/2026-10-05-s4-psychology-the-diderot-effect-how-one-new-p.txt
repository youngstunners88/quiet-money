In 1765, a philosopher got a free robe and went completely broke. His name was Denis Diderot, and his mistake is quietly costing you thousands of dollars today. Diderot was poor, but then he received an incredibly beautiful scarlet robe as a gift. It was gorgeous. But suddenly, his old wooden desk looked too cheap to match it. So he bought a new leather desk. Then an expensive clock. Then luxury chairs. But here's the part nobody mentions about these upgrades. Every single new item demanded another matching upgrade, and he fell into deep debt. Modern psychologists call this the Diderot Effect. One single purchase triggers ten more. It's why a new phone requires a new case, a wireless charger, and Bluetooth headphones. These small, seemingly harmless daily upgrades quickly turn into a $5/day habit. That's $1,825 a year, which invested at 8% for 30 years grows to $226,659. Never buy things just to match other things. You are serving the object, not yourself.
