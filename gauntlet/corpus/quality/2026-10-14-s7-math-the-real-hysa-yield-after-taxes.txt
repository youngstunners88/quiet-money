You put $10,000 into a 4.5% high-yield savings account. It feels safe. You expect $450 in interest after 1 year. But the math hides a trap. First, the IRS takes its cut. At a 22% tax bracket, you lose $99. That leaves you with $351 in actual cash gain. Your balance is now $10,351. But here is the part nobody mentions. Inflation is eating your baseline. At 3% inflation, the cost of everything you buy goes up. That $10,351 needs significantly more purchasing power just to stay even. Adjusted for inflation, your real buying power drops below where you started. You earned interest on paper, but your actual purchasing power shrank. Cash in a savings account keeps you safe from drops, not from loss. Use savings for short-term cash only. Grow long-term wealth elsewhere. Follow for the money rules school never taught you.
