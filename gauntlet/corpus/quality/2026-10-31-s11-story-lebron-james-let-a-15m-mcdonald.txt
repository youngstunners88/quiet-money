A basketball star reportedly walked away from fifteen million dollars of burger money. In twenty fifteen LeBron James let his McDonald's deal lapse. According to Forbes, his partner said that meant about fourteen to fifteen million dollars over four years. Why say no to that? Because he had picked a different kind of payment. Three years earlier, he put about one million dollars of his own money into a small pizza chain called Blaze. Reports say that bought him more than ten percent of the company. Here is the part nobody tells you. A paycheck pays once. A piece of a company keeps growing while you sleep. So he stopped selling his name for a fee and started trading it for ownership. Forbes estimated his Blaze stake at about thirty million dollars in twenty twenty two. A one million dollar bet, estimated at thirty. Against a reported fifteen million he turned down. Now the honest catch. He was already one of the highest paid athletes alive, and he used his own cash. Most people are never offered a stake, and plenty of equity deals pay nothing. We only hear about winners. But the question travels. Next time you are paid for your work, ask what a piece of the result would be worth. Follow for the money rules school never taught you.
