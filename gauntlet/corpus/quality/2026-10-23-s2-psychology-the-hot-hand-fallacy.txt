Winning once makes your brain feel invincible, setting you up to lose everything on the very next roll of the dice. That single lucky win in business or trading triggers a cognitive glitch known as the hot-hand fallacy. Why does a random streak of luck convince smart people to risk their entire net worth on the next gamble? Most people assume a hot streak means your odds of winning keep getting better with every single wager. That assumption is entirely false. Independent events never remember what happened on the turn before. When you double down after a win, the mathematical house edge hasn't changed at all. How does that overconfidence quietly drain an entire savings account in a single afternoon? The brain treats dopamine from a random win the same way it treats proven skill. Instead of cashing out, you take on massive leverage, treating your luck as a permanent asset. Real wealth isn't built on hot streaks. It's built on boring consistency like a $200 monthly investment compounding at 8% a year. Ignore the illusion of the hot hand. Protect your cash flow and build real staying power.
