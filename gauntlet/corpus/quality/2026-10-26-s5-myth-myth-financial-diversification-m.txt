Owning twenty different funds doesn't mean your hard-earned money is safe. Most investors buy five index funds, assuming they've spread their risk. So why does a single market downturn still wipe out their entire portfolio? You assume you own hundreds of companies, but you bought the same ten giants five times. Wall Street calls this diworsification: it concentrates your risk instead of spreading it. When your broad, tech and growth funds hold identical shares, you aren't hedged. You haven't built a safety net; you've just bought multiple tickets on the same sinking ship. Worse, overlapping funds add expensive management fees every year. $100,000 for 30 years at 7% minus a 1.00% annual fee leaves you with $574,349. With a 0.05% annual fee, that exact same $100,000 grows to $750,626. That overlap doesn't protect you; it just burns $176,277 in lost gains for fake safety. Real diversification means different asset classes, not identical stocks in separate wrappers. Cut the duplicates, lower your fees, and follow for the money rules school never taught you.
