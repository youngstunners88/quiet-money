In 2015, LeBron James walked away from a reported fifteen million dollars to back a pizza chain. McDonald's had paid him about four million dollars a year. Another four years meant roughly fifteen million, his business partner says. So he backed a small pizza chain instead, with about a million dollars of his own. A flat fee ends when the contract ends. A stake keeps growing with the company. ESPN reported he owns more than ten percent of Blaze. So what was that stake worth? By 2022, Forbes estimated it at about thirty million dollars, roughly thirty times his first investment, on paper. In 2008 he took a small stake in Beats for promoting the headphones. Was that a fluke? Apple bought Beats for three billion dollars in 2014, and he reportedly collected about thirty million. So why choose a stake over guaranteed money? A check pays once. Ownership keeps paying as the company grows. The catch: he was already among the league's highest-paid players, and most equity deals pay nothing. Check or stake?
