In 2005, a struggling graffiti artist was offered either $60,000 in cash or company shares to paint walls. Most folks would grab the $60,000. That's a safe paycheck you can spend today. David Choe didn't want the safe cash. He took a gamble on stock in a tiny startup called Facebook. Why would anyone trade guaranteed money for random paper? How did that wild bet actually play out? You assume a small art gig pays pennies. But his risk wasn't blind luck. His baseline living costs were covered. When you don't need the next paycheck to survive, you can play a totally different game than everyone else. That graffiti gig turned into shares worth $200,000,000 when the company went public. Linear earned income has a hard ceiling. Asymmetric equity risk has mathematically infinite upside. Cover your baseline costs first. Then take the equity. Follow for the money rules school never taught you.
