In 2007, Warren Buffett made a public bet against Wall Street's finest minds. He wagered $1 million that a simple index fund would beat any handpicked portfolio of hedge funds. The hedge fund managers laughed all the way to their high-rise offices. They charged massive fees to deploy complex trading strategies and elite mathematical models. Buffett picked a boring fund that just tracked the S&P 500 index. For ten full years, the clock ticked down on their high-stakes financial duel. But here's the part nobody mentions about how Wall Street quietly bleeds your returns dry. While the experts took their 2% management fees win or lose, the index fund charged almost nothing. When the decade ended in 2017, the results shocked the entire financial establishment. The hedge funds returned a meager 22% total over the ten-year span. Buffett's boring index fund crushed them with an 85% return. Simplicity and low fees beat genius every single time, and that's exactly how you should follow for the money rules school never taught you.
