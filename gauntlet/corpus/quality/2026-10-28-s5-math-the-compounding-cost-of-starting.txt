Waiting just ten years to start investing doesn't cost you ten years of savings—it costs you half a million dollars by the time you retire. How does pausing for a single decade erase a fortune? Most people assume waiting till thirty just lops off the exact cash they failed to stash away. That is completely wrong. Let's run the exact math at an 8% return until age 65. If you invest $200 a month starting at age 20, you put in $96,000 over your working life. Compound interest turns that pile into a massive $698,202 by the time you hit 65. Now look what happens if you wait until age 30 to drop that exact same $200 a month. You only put in $72,000, which sounds close enough. Except your total crashes down to $298,072. Delaying ten years strips $400,130 straight out of your future account. Time is the only asset you cannot buy back. Follow for the money rules school never taught you.
