A janitor in Vermont died in 2014 with almost $8 million. And nobody knew. Not his friends. Not his neighbors. A diner stranger once paid for his meal because he looked broke. His name was Ronald Read. He pumped gas for 25 years, then cleaned floors at J.C. Penney. He held his jacket together with a safety pin. Drove a used Toyota. So how did a man with no big salary build $8 million? Three boring rules. One. He only bought boring companies he understood. Soap, medicine, electricity. Two. Every dividend they paid him bought more shares. Bigger dividends bought even more. A snowball that never stopped rolling. Three. He almost never sold. His stock certificates made a stack five inches tall. You don't need his 50-plus years. $200 a month at 8% a year for 40 years is about $698,000. Not a promise. Just math. He left $4.8 million to the local hospital and $1.2 million to the library. No big salary. No genius trades. Just time, patience, and a safety pin. Follow for money rules school never taught you.
