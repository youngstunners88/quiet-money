At fifteen, Taylor Swift signed a deal that meant she did not own her own songs. Her label kept the master recordings. That was the standard deal. She was a teenager, and she signed. In twenty nineteen the label was sold, and her first six albums went with it, for around three hundred million dollars. She says she asked for years for a chance to own her work, and never got one she could take. So what does a person do when someone else owns the thing they made? Here is the part nobody tells you. She kept the publishing, because she wrote the songs. So she could record them again. She re-recorded her old albums as Taylor's Version. Fans streamed the new ones. In twenty twenty the catalog was resold, reportedly for three hundred million dollars or more. In May twenty twenty five, she announced she had bought her masters back. Price undisclosed, but reports say nine figures. Six years. A public fight. Hundreds of millions of dollars. To own what she wrote at fifteen. The honest catch: her fame made this possible. Most artists never get that chance. The lesson is sharper. Before you sign, read who owns the work you make, and what has to happen for it to come back. Follow for the money rules school never taught you.
