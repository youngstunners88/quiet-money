Same $300 a month at 24%: $3,000 costs $381 in interest. $6,000 costs $1,739. You doubled the balance, but your interest bill jumped more than 4 times. Why? Most people assume debt math moves in a straight line. Double the card, double the penalty. But fixed monthly payments don't work like that when interest charges eat your cash first. Take the $3,000 balance at 24% APR. Paying $300 a month clears it in 12 months. You hand over $3,381 total. Only $381 goes to the bank. Now double that balance to $6,000, keeping the exact same $300 monthly check. You'd expect it to take 24 months and cost double the interest, around $760. It actually takes 26 months. And the interest rockets straight to $1,739. That fixed $300 payment gets eaten alive by interest early on, dragging out your timeline. Small payment increases protect your paycheck before larger balances multiply your total cost. Follow Quiet Money for the simple money rules school never taught you about your card.
