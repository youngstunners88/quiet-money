You remember knowing before the drop. You don't remember not knowing. That trade you missed. That stock you swore you called. Your brain rewrites the past. Why does your memory instantly fabricate old certainty about money? You assume your financial intuition is sharp, because you recall predicting every major market shift. The truth is, once an outcome is known, your brain erases your actual past confusion. It weaves the final result into your memory as if it was obvious all along. How much cash does this mental trick cost you every year? You think you're getting wiser at spotting trends. So you take bigger risks with your paycheck. You bet $500 a month on complex trades, thinking your hindsight foresight makes you immune to losses. Instead of steady investing at 8% a year, you chase ghosts in your own memory. Stop trusting a memory that edits itself after the fact. Write down your bets before they happen. Follow for the money rules school never taught you.
