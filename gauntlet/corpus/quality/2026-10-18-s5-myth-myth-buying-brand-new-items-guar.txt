Buy anything brand new, and you instantly burn twenty percent of its value. Most people call that the price of reliability, but the real math tells a completely different story. We crave pristine condition because our psychology mistakes untouched packaging for long-term safety and superior value. In reality, that shiny factory seal only gives you a dopamine rush for five minutes. It's the depreciation cliff. The steepest loss always hits between the showroom floor and owner one. Here's the part that nobody ever mentions. A two-year-old certified item delivers ninety-nine percent of the utility, after someone else ate the massive loss. Avoiding that brand new markup easily frees up an extra $200 a month in your budget. That $200 a month invested for 20 years at 8% a year compounds into $117,804. Keep going for 30 years at 8%: you put in $72,000, and it reaches $298,072. The replacement rule's simple: pay strictly for utility, never pay for the temporary thrill of unboxing. Let other people absorb the depreciation hit while you direct that difference into compounding assets. Follow for the money rules school never taught you, before buying your next brand new item.
