In 1988, a penniless carnival worker holding just $2 won a $16 million lottery jackpot. Within three months, he wasn't wealthy. He was already drowning in $500,000 of fresh debt. His name was William Post. How does someone burn through eight figures in under a year? Most people assume he blew every dime on luxury vacations, wild parties, and gold watches. That wasn't what ruined him. The instant his check cleared, everyone in his life turned hostile. His own brother hired a contract killer to murder him and steal the remaining payouts. Then an ex-landlord sued him for a third of the fortune, successfully freezing his bank accounts. Panicked and isolated, Post bought failing restaurants, used-car lots, and an airplane he couldn't fly. Within twelve months, he owed over $1 million, facing bankruptcy and a prison sentence over gunfire. Sudden wealth doesn't cure poor financial discipline. It acts as an amplifier for every personal weakness. Post spent his final years surviving on a modest $450 monthly disability check in rural seclusion. Before he died, he insisted he was vastly happier back when he was completely broke. If you don't build money systems when you're small, millions will only crush you faster. Follow for the money rules school never taught you.
