If you spend $500 on monthly groceries, nearly 30% ends up in the trash. That's over $1,800 thrown directly into the dumpster every single year. How did 1930s families cut food costs without starving during an economic collapse? Most people assume they just ate less food or skipped daily meals. But Depression-era households survived by adopting a commercial store rule: First-In, First-Out. Supermarkets still use it today to protect profits, while your home fridge stays chaotic. Step one: when unpacking bags, pull older ingredients to the front of your shelves. Step two: load fresh groceries directly behind the old items so they're hidden. Why does this basic 1930s pantry rotation stop you from wasting hard-earned cash? Because pushing older food front and center eliminates forgotten rot, duplicate purchases, and expensive takeout. You end up eating every single dollar you spend instead of throwing cash away. It's the simplest habit to cut grocery bills without eating less daily food. Follow for the money rules school never taught you, and stop wasting your paycheck.
