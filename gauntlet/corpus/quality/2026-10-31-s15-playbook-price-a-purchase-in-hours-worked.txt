A twelve hundred dollar phone is not twelve hundred dollars. It is forty eight hours of your life. There is a simple rule. Before any purchase, divide the price by what you earn in an hour after tax. Say you take home twenty five dollars an hour. That is your exchange rate between money and time. Twelve hundred divided by twenty five is forty eight. Six full workdays for one phone. Does that change how it feels? For some, yes. For others, the phone is still worth it. Both are fine. Now try something small. A five dollar coffee every workday is about twelve hundred fifty dollars a year. Here is the part nobody tells you. That is fifty hours of work a year. More than a full workweek, on one habit. Now flip it. A three thousand dollar vacation is one hundred twenty hours. Fifteen workdays for a trip you will remember. This rule does not tell you to stop spending. It tells you what you are actually trading. So here is the move. Find your hourly number tonight. Write it on a card in your wallet. Next time you hover over the buy button, divide first. Then decide. Spend on what is worth your hours. Skip what is not. Follow for the money rules school never taught you.
