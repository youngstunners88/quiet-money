Keeping your life savings in cash isn't safe; it's a guaranteed loss. Here's the exact math that destroys your purchasing power, and the simple rule to stop it. Most people keep everything in checking accounts because seeing a steady balance feels comforting. Your account balance never drops on paper, so you feel completely protected from risk. Inflation doesn't steal your paper bills; it quietly drains what those bills can buy. At just 3% inflation, $100 today is worth $55 in buying power after 20 years. But here's the part nobody mentions. While you avoided market dips, you locked in a permanent 45% drop in real wealth. Cash is a tool for short-term liquidity, not a vehicle for long-term storage. Wealthy people hold three to six months of expenses in cash, then deploy the rest. They hold broad-market index funds and productive assets that naturally outpace rising living costs. Invested monthly at 8% a year, even $100 a month reaches $58,902 in 20 years. Drop it in the comments: how many months of cash do you hold right now? Follow for the money rules school never taught you, and stop letting inflation quietly bleed your savings.
