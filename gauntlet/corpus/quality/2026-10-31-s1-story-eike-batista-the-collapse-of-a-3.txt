In 2012, Eike Batista held a $35 billion net worth—18 months later, he owed $1 billion. He was the world's seventh-richest man, publicly vowing to overtake Bill Gates as number one. How does a $35 billion fortune evaporate into negative net worth in under two years? Most assume a sudden stock market crash or catastrophic legal fine wiped him out. That's not what happened. His $35 billion empire was an illusion from day one. Batista sold stock in oil fields that hadn't pumped a single barrel of oil yet. Investors inflated his paper valuation based on purely speculative geological projections and heavy borrowing. But what happens when debt comes due and the math collides with real life? When production started, actual oil yield was a fraction of what he promised investors. Stock prices plummeted 99%, triggering billions in debt calls he couldn't cover with real cash. Paper net worth isn't real money. Wealth requires tangible, cash-flowing assets that survive bad years. Never mistake high paper valuations for financial freedom. Follow for the money rules school never taught you, so you never build on paper promises.
